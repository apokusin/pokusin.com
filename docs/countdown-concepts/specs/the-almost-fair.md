# The Almost Fair — world-first revision

This direction has been rebuilt as a fully inhabited installation. The earlier page-and-hero implementation is retained as a reference, but its visual acceptance is superseded by the user's rejection of traditional web layout and the explicit request to recover the reference's material and lighting richness. This brief supersedes conflicting instructions in the shared guide for this concept. No final collection-wide theme has been selected.

Read the [revised art-director report](../reports/the-almost-fair.md) and [reference](../references/the-almost-fair.jpg). The reference is an art target, not a flat background image or a specification for invented archive content. Numerical render settings below distinguish implemented source settings from targets. Final desktop and 390 px runtime captures have passed the revised visual-direction review; this is acceptance of the world-first slice, not a claim of photographic equivalence with the generated reference. Runtime/failure checks remain separately recorded in the art report and shared QA.

### Premise

A small, impossibly well-prepared fairground waits to open. Visitors explore Artur's actual countdown sites as exhibits inside a tactile architectural world. The fair's giant clock gate counts toward its opening; operating a ridiculous postponement machine moves the shared deadline another month ahead. The world quietly prepares itself again. No objectives, quests, simulated visitors, or progress to complete are needed. The joke should be legible from the operation and its consequences, with few words.

The world is the site. Its viewport is continuous and full-screen; it does not sit above a conventional archive page. Existing other explorations remain accessible as references through a physical gateway. The ordinary archive is a failure/accessibility route rather than the default visual continuation of the installation.

### Art style and composition

Restore the reference's tactile miniature architecture and cinematic staging while preserving low-poly silhouettes and the simple sphere-headed figure. This is an authored, sculptural fairground rather than a game asset town or a rack of rectangular thumbnails. The first frame has a back-facing character in the lower third, a cream path widening into a clock court, the clock machine just beyond, and two substantially different exhibit silhouettes to either side. A partial foreground pedestal and a distant pavilion create overlap. The distant landscape and sky establish depth beyond the collection's seven exhibits. Use full-viewport composition with no masthead, page title, show tabs, website container, conventional footer, or fixed clock/button HUD.

A 38 × 30 unit fairground and figure-eight route remain useful starting dimensions. Paths are continuous, at least 2.4 units wide, and flush or ramped at every destination. A screen's visible architectural surround must include depth: threshold, frame, canopy, shaded recess, or supported mechanism. Each exhibit gets a different silhouette and operation. Avoid seven identical screen kiosks. Keep sightlines deliberately open between major landmarks; occasional foreground occlusion is desirable, trapping the camera behind walls is not. A small number of low-poly, clustered foliage masses and layered cloud forms should restore the reference's atmosphere; they are compositional background, not scatter decoration.

### Palette and typography

Keep cream `#F2E5CC`, jade `#77958A`, coral `#D86B55`, and charcoal `#263B40` as the main material families, with lighter cream, deep jade recesses, and desaturated blue-gray sky derived from them. Color must vary through light, roughness and surface treatment rather than becoming one flat paint value per mesh. Archive screenshots retain their original colors.

Render physical clock digits and small plaques on surfaces. The rebuilt drum faces, unit marks, engraved Again and tally use Georgia, matching the reference's quieter serif numerals; the existing IBM Plex Mono remains available to the semantic/fallback layer. Typography is drawn from the existing live values and reel plan, so changing the visible face does not change numerical accuracy. Labels should be show/version names, D/H/M/S, Again, and a small press tally where needed. No explanatory copy walls. The title is embodied by the clock gate; there is no large flat Countdown heading across the sky. Portal and shortcut labels appear only when their physical target is approached or focused.

### Geometry and materials

Use bevels on plinths, posts, frame rims and clock casing so light can describe thickness. Bevel size should remain consistent with the miniature scale, approximately 0.015–0.04 units on architecture. Thin cloth roofs have actual scalloped edges and ribbed panels. Ceramic or painted-plaster surfaces get slight mottled albedo, a restrained fine bump and roughness variation. Painted timber shows modeled joints and tiny edge wear; cloth receives broad folds and a barely visible weave. These are material-specific effects, not full-screen grain or the same noise on every object. Start with 256–512 px repeatable procedural material textures and 2–5% albedo variation. Never recolor the archived work or introduce photorealistic noisy grasses.

The character stays anonymous: sphere head, soft low-poly coral torso, short charcoal feet, no face or costume selection. Its torso should have subtle surface texture and a rounded shoulder silhouette, avoiding the current rigid trapezoid. GoT receives a sculptural canopy with scalloped cloth and hinged folios; Dexter has a coral tiled court and episode rail; Sherlock has a deep arch with inclined reading desks; Archer has a deliberately operable rotating easel carousel; Breaking Bad has an angular folded canopy; House of Cards has a layered folded-plane pavilion; Severance has clean, deeply recessed jade terminals. These are seven variations on a coherent material culture, not seven copies of one display stand. Actual faithful artwork is mounted inside their real recesses.

The current material helper uses packed 256 px world-projected procedural textures with separate albedo, roughness and height channels. Its source recipe distinguishes limestone (roughness 0.85), plaster (0.90), cloth (0.92), terracotta (0.65), jade painted surfaces (0.70), charcoal enamel (0.38, metalness 0.12), and a restrained warm trim (0.42, metalness 0.25). These are implemented source settings, not measured surface or performance claims. The material types must remain visibly distinct under the final lighting.

The clock is mechanical geometry: rounded housing, supported drums, axle ends, rim bevels, and a visibly connected reset machine. Physical drum textures mirror the real shared timer; keep a single accessible DOM representation and the existing timer/reel model. Do not calculate a second decorative deadline. The slice implements the actual engraved press tally, cap compression, reel rewind and small pennant response. The latest machinery source also adds one reusable paper chit printed with the confirmed press number: it ejects, bends, retracts and hides within 1.35 seconds, rather than accumulating objects. Reduced motion suppresses the chit entirely. Root verified a real successful press from 371 to 372 and the numbered chit response; the supplied runtime capture visibly shows ticket 372 above the compressed machine while the physical drums spin, and root observed it retracting within the bounded duration. A persistent tray of counted objects remains an optional later direction.

### Camera and lighting

Aim for the reference's warmly lit miniature under a cool sky. Use a warm key from upper left and front, a cool hemisphere fill, restrained contact occlusion and a much weaker back/edge fill if needed to separate the cloth silhouette. Starting key direction `(-7,10,8)` and approximately 4000–4500 K is more important than a literal intensity. Tune the fill around 0.5–0.8 relative to a 2–3 key so recesses retain depth without becoming black. Cream must read as warm plaster; jade shaded facets must stay cool; coral must retain light on its bevels. Avoid a uniformly lit green void. Stage broad shadows across the promenade, with penumbra and small contact shadows anchoring feet, screens, steps, posts and machine parts. Use restrained modeled or texture contact AO around 12–20% darkening; it must not become a black contour around every object. The rebuilt source currently uses Neutral tone mapping at exposure 1.05, a warm `#fff2de` directional key at intensity 2.65 from `(-22,30,21)` aimed toward `(0,0,-3)`, cool `#d3e0e8` / muted ground hemisphere fill at 0.57, and a cool rim at 0.28. A generated broad sky reflection uses environment intensity 0.34; it is procedural, without a photographic HDR dependency. The shadow map is 2048 desktop / 1024 phone, with PCF soft shadows and radius 3. Background-only aerial separation uses fog distances 29–77. These source values document the current recipe; actual captures determine whether the warm/cool separation and contact remain convincing.

Begin with a 44–48° perspective lens, character in the lower third, clock crown below the upper frame, and two distinct exhibits visible on desktop. The original implemented 48° lens and 6.5-unit follow distance are only starting points. Portrait framing may pull to 7.5 units and raise pitch; it must preserve the figure, next path and machine together rather than shrink the whole fair. Camera follow has roughly 120 ms response, no head bob, horizon roll, idle orbit, or automatic yaw. Direct focus approaches an exhibit over 450–650 ms on a clear authored path, brakes gently, then settles facing its actual aperture. Save the exact prior pose for return. Reduced motion cuts directly between these deliberate poses. Do not use a broad lens, high-angle overview or fake miniature blur merely to fit everything on screen.

### Interaction contract

Prioritize five physical operations, in this order:

1. **Walk the prepared route.** WASD/arrows and click-to-walk move the little figure; drag orbits around it. The path, gate opening, thresholds and supported fronts show where to go. Movement begins only after deliberate input. A 6 px mouse / 10 px touch drag threshold consumes release so orbit never accidentally activates an exhibit. Controls beginning a gesture never become locomotion. The authored route remains obstacle-aware and movement cancels on blur, pointer cancellation, visibility change or inspection.
2. **Postpone the opening.** Activate the coral machine itself by raycast, touch, or its semantic keyboard equivalent. Its cap/lever compresses while the real request is pending. Confirmed success rewinds the actual clock drums and rolls the physical tally, with a small connected pennant response. The machinery ejects one bounded numbered paper chit after confirmation; actual reset/capture verification establishes its visible response. A more elaborate folded-back opening banner remains optional. Failure releases the handle without a ticket or rewind. This physical state change carries the joke; there is no fixed HTML reset button floating above it.
3. **Inspect a real exhibit.** A broad supported frontage is the activation target, not a tiny pixel inside a thumbnail. Approach opens its leaf/canopy enough to reveal a warm recess, and keyboard focus applies the same physical acknowledgment immediately. Deliberate activation approaches the camera into the exhibit's projector aperture; the real preserved page then operates inside that aperture. The iframe is a DOM layer physically registered to the scene, not a generic centered modal with a card border, backdrop and toolbar. Use camera alignment or a CSS 3D transform tied to the visible frame; reserve the physical surround and readable iframe area on phone. Escape or one quiet close affordance in the frame returns to the exact player/camera pose and restores focus, with no resumed walking. Modified/middle activation still opens the genuine URL.
4. **Turn the exhibit mechanism.** Variant selection belongs to each object: turn an easel, hinge a folio, or move a finite timeline carriage. One deliberate operation reveals the next faithful archived version and its short plaque; do not display all versions as a card wall. Each mechanism is bounded, has a visible hinge/axle/track, and stops before the visitor reads. A semantic direct-access equivalent can focus the same physical item and enter inspection without requiring walking or motor precision. In the accepted slice, mounted representative versions open directly, and a small cabinet latch on every show with further variants or a timeline opens its genuine archive page. That preserves access to the complete collection; cycling every collapsed variant inside a pavilion remains a future interaction direction.
5. **Use a spatial shortcut.** A hinged plan at the arrival court or small constellation gateway gives direct access to all seven shows and retained Worlds. The plan is a miniature of the actual layout, and gateway objects use distinct silhouettes from their referenced theme—bubble, droplet, leaf, suspended ring, folded sheet—rather than thumbnails or text rows. Activating one focuses/travels to a safe facing pose or navigates to its genuine theme URL. The implemented focused constellation reveals ten readable names beside distinct miniature objects: nine retained themes plus Home. It remains hidden in the ordinary world, and the physical gateway first approaches this view. Tab focus brings the corresponding gateway/object into view before activation. This is a destination encounter rather than a persistent labelled button wall. The gateway must be visibly distinct from the archive exhibits so clicking it does not surprise visitors with an exit.

Preserve exactly one hidden crown embossing inside the clock's lower inner gate, discoverable from a slight side approach or explicit focus. Activating it reveals Long may I count once, near the clue. It has tap/Enter access, with an optional hold as an extra interaction only. No collectible objective, crown count or second hidden joke is required. All semantic controls should be available to assistive technology, but rendering them as conventional visible buttons is not required in the working scene.

### Motion choreography

The first frame is settled: drifting cloth tips and very slow distant clouds are enough. Nothing loops a camera tour, bounces every target, emits cursor particles, or asks for attention with periodic motion. Walk speed starts around 3 units/second, acceleration 150 ms, braking 90 ms and body turn 180 ms; feet articulate only during movement. Hand/torso acknowledgment can make operating the machine feel intentional without a character cutscene.

Exhibit approach opens one supported leaf 8–12° over 280 ms, with a 400 ms close. Focus under reduced motion shows its readable state immediately. Carousel turns should be deliberate, quantized and finite: roughly 60° in 700–900 ms, then quiet. On confirmed reset, the implemented machine drives the drums and tally and briefly dips its pennants. The latest source ejects, bends and retracts one reusable numbered paper chit over 1.35 seconds after confirmation, with no chit under reduced motion. The confirmed press-number capture and root's retraction observation verify this bounded choreography. A prepared opening ribbon is optional and must also have one bounded response rather than inventing an unlimited accumulation. Reels retain their existing 1080 ms spin with 45 ms column staggering, settling on the current real value. A remote press produces only a small clock acknowledgment; it never moves the visitor or invents a visible visitor. No success ceremony runs while a request is merely pending.

### Effects and render budget

Spend rendering effort on depth, materials and the mechanical landmark before particle effects. Budget one main WebGL canvas, one tightly bounded shadow-casting directional light, 2048² shadow resolution on desktop / 1024² on phone as starting values, approximately 80–120 draw calls and 100,000 visible triangles. Instance repeated trim, roof ribs and background foliage. Use bounded reusable material textures and ticket/token buffers. These are targets to profile, not measured performance claims. Cap DPR at 1.5 desktop / 1.25 phone. No physics engine, multiplayer simulation, bloom wash, chromatic aberration, or full-screen fake grain is needed.

Use a sky gradient plus genuinely spatial cloud volumes/planes and a restrained distant low-poly landscape. Aerial color separation may soften the far background; do not fog away the exhibits or apply screen-space haze to the live iframe. Ambient contact shading and bevel highlights should survive at 320 px. Decorative animation pauses when hidden; explicit movement can still request frames under reduced motion. Dispose geometry/textures/listeners on theme exit and release all controls on context loss.

### Responsive and fallback behavior

The healthy world fills `100svh` and has no below-fold gallery continuation. The accepted 390 px first frame concentrates on the character, clock and machine; the physical plan top and complete Worlds ring/core remain recognizable in the foreground, with minor plinth-edge cropping used as spatial overlap. Plan inspection then fills the phone with its whole operative surface; projector inspection fills a readable physical aperture. Do not require every exhibit to appear simultaneously in the portrait spawn. On phone, a small left thumb control and a right orbit area are the only persistent functional overlays needed; they are gesture affordances rather than a HUD. Touch input stops on release/cancellation and supports movement plus camera orbit simultaneously. The machine and exhibit target areas remain generous. An inspecting iframe receives a readable portrait aperture and a close target at least 44 px, with its 3D frame remaining visible.

Keyboard users can discover every object through semantic focus. Focus must correspond to a visible scene response and camera framing; avoid invisible targets whose Tab sequence disappears beyond the camera. The implemented plan uses distinguishable cone, block and round token silhouettes on the real route diagram; the focused view enlarges them before selection. A quiet accessibility/direct-access route may present genuine links when explicitly requested or when graphics fail. It is not the default layout. On WebGL failure/context loss, display the illustrated ground plan, real countdown/reset/status and every genuine version link; omit unavailable walking and travel controls. The ordinary archive may return in this failure state. Reduced motion freezes idle texture/cloth/cloud movement, cuts requested focus/travel, and updates numerical values immediately while preserving deliberate walking and orbit.

### Implementation boundaries

Remain static, using the generator, vendored Three.js, existing digit reels, shared API and faithful archive pages. No new framework, package manifest, build step, server state, account, inventory, or multiplayer claims. Keep the original other themes as comparison references. Root runtime may extend the previous concept contract to permit a full-viewport world and physically registered live projector rather than the generic archive overlay. DOM remains the semantic and live-content layer; Three.js owns almost all perceptual presence. Do not move the API, recreate its timer state, or replace preserved sites with imitations.

This revision intentionally overrides the older blanket restrictions against texture, shaped landscape/clouds, cloth scallops, architectural bevels and visible surface character. Keep them coherent and restrained, as the reference does. Step-free routes still take priority over the reference's decorative stairs. A real iframe cannot be rendered as a normal WebGL texture without a different content pipeline; solve its registration with the scene honestly rather than pretending a static screenshot is interactive.

### Fidelity checks

Accept the new implementation only after reviewing actual first-frame desktop and 390/320 px renders against the reference. The screenshot should read as an inviting tactile place, with warm sunlight, cool recessed surfaces, bevel highlights, material distinction, foreground overlap, character and clock together. There must be no visible conventional header, show-tab strip, fixed clock/button UI, archive card continuation or generic website panel. In-frame clocks and controls must still be actual shared state.

An unfamiliar visitor should find a reachable exhibit and machine from their geometry and response. First drag must orbit without activation; walking must stop promptly. Inspecting must load the genuine archive inside the scene's projector aperture, remain usable, and return to the exact saved pose/focus. Every version and theme must remain reachable through the physical direct-access mechanism or semantic equivalent. Successful shared reset increments once and changes the actual deadline; pending/error/429 leave it unchanged. Reduced motion, keyboard, simultaneous phone gestures, repeated resets, graphics loss and cancellation must remain stable. Do not mark this direction passed from screenshots alone: the camera transition, material response and input cancellation define its feel.
