# Architecture reset: three installations

Prepared October 2, 2026, after the user rejected the seven non-Fair implementations. This is an architectural replacement proposal, not acceptance of their current appearance. The Fair remains unchanged. The original reference images remain the targets; the subsequent material screenshots demonstrate the failure rather than a successful interpretation.

## The shared mistake

The runtime starts from an HTML hero, three relocated cards, upright DOM numbers and a scrolling gallery continuation. Each artist is then asked to decorate that fixed arrangement. `ctx.pin` projects a point and a world-X width; it does not give the HTML surface the mesh's orientation, perspective, curvature, depth occlusion or illumination. A curled sheet can move underneath a screenshot that remains a frontal rectangle. A metal plate can swing while its numerals appear stuck to the browser. The scene and its contents consequently read as different media.

The three representative mounts also force a sparse three-card composition where the reference contains an entire exhibition. The remaining archive becomes conventional shelves below the picture. Painting those shelves in thematic colors does not make them part of the artwork. A generated coast placed behind simple foreground geometry compounds the problem: its different detail density, shadows and camera cannot be reconciled by increasing bump-map strength.

Orthographic projection is not intrinsically the mistake. It is appropriate for an overhead animator's table and can work for a miniature. The failure is a generic camera and HTML arrangement dictating every concept, rather than the concept determining its complete scene and navigation.

### Replacement interface

- A concept owns a complete viewport installation, its camera positions, its spatial archive organization and its visual controls. There is no required hero/three-card/shelf arrangement while graphics are healthy.
- The common runtime supplies real countdown/reel state, reset requests, all seven show groups and all 13 visible archive versions, actual thumbnail files, resource/lifecycle handling, and the genuine preview modal. It does not decide where they appear.
- Closed archive work is a faithful texture on an actual mesh. Digits, tally and sparse labels are textures attached to their material surfaces, driven by the existing reel model. Accessible values remain immediate in one semantic DOM representation.
- Native links/buttons remain a semantic twin. Keyboard focus must frame and mark the associated object. Pointer/touch activation raycasts the visible exhibit or physical action, with deliberate target padding. Touch cancellation and preview opening cancel all dragging.
- Only an opened, approached, planar screen needs a live iframe. The adapter fits that screen's projected aperture or uses the existing readable modal when a concept explicitly calls for it. A permanent upright DOM card is not a scene surface.
- All versions have real URLs and native equivalents; modified/middle clicks retain new-tab behavior. Graphics failure restores the existing illustrated DOM archive. Healthy graphics may visually clip this twin, as the Fair already does.
- Asset readiness is explicit. Structural geometry and a coherent base material appear together; optional microtexture loads never substitute black surfaces. A concept cannot become “ready” merely because its HTML controls loaded.

### Complete auxiliary-link inventory

The seven show groups contain 13 gallery works; those are not the entire link contract. Preserve the five existing More destinations (`/countdowns/got/`, `/countdowns/dexter/`, `/countdowns/sherlock/`, `/countdowns/archer/`, `/countdowns/breaking-bad/`) with the selected theme, Dexter's actual `/countdowns/dexter/s7-episodes/` timeline and the real `https://severancecountdown.com` live-site link. Home/Worlds also remain directly available. Non-live former-domain labels stay labels rather than being turned into dead outgoing links.

## Held in Suspense

Reference: [original installation](../references/held-in-suspense.jpg). Rejected result: [material revision](../qa/held-in-suspense-material-desktop.jpg).

### Reference commitments and current failure

The source is a monumental counterbalanced mechanism photographed inside a sunlit architectural room. Two continuous folded steel arms rise from a rough, heavy stone base. Their long slopes establish the composition. Four tall numeral plates hang below the lower arm; three unequal archive plates hang under the upper arm. A large machined cylinder is suspended at the right. Its polished elliptical face, weight, cables and floor contact explain the Again action. Black filings occupy a patch of the foreground floor.

The current result looks like a rail diagram. The plates and screenshot faces are frontal, their visible lower label strips resemble card UI, the room is a uniform off-white void, the floor is matte grey, and the thick black beam sides overwhelm weak metallic reflection. Numeral plates are square-ish and use a different typographic silhouette. Pivots read as identical outlined circles rather than different mechanical connections. Texture additions cannot correct these structural differences.

### Scene and composition

Build the entire mechanism as one articulated scene graph. The stone base is a fractured tapered volume, approximately one third the height of a numeral plate, with a separately bolted mounting shoe. The lower arm is a continuous bent rectangular section, broad enough to reflect the window, with rounded folded elbows. The upper arm climbs diagonally before descending to the right counterweight pulley. Arms must have actual thickness, bevels and distinct front/side surfaces; no disconnected skinny rods tracing the outline.

The main opening view follows the original hierarchy: base near the lower-left, upper arm reaching near the top edge, archive plates across the upper field, four long clock plates across the lower-middle, weight low-right, and a foreground floor area below them. Leave room above and behind the installation so the silhouette remains architectural rather than a diagram squeezed against the viewport. Clock plates use the reference's tall condensed numerals, approximately 1:2 in the reference projection face proportion, thin D/H/M/S engraving below, and small unequal fasteners. Place numeral ink on their actual front faces.

Model the weight as a real cylindrical part at an oblique angle. Its action face remains perpendicular to its own axis, not turned toward the browser independently of the body. Again and the tally are engraved/printed on that face. Taut twin cables meet a clevis and actual axle. Bolts, collars and flange rings differ by their function. Archive images have a thin physical steel lip, with any show name engraved on that lip or backplate; no HTML card footer.

### Camera, navigation and archive

Use a modest perspective camera, roughly 38–45° vertical field of view, from a three-quarter position above the floor. Tune the opening projection against the reference before choosing exact coordinates. There is no automatic orbit and no free-flight camera. The camera remains quiet while gravity supplies motion.

The installation extends beyond the first three archive plates along its upper hanging rail. Seven show positions follow a horizontal/spatial rail; a low visual ruler at the left indicates the current position through its moving long tick, without adding a navigation panel. Wheel/trackpad or drag on the empty room moves along this rail; touch swipes do the same. Selecting a visible show plate brings its face forward without moving the pointer target before activation. Native show links directly select the corresponding camera stop. The opening view still contains the entire clock and reset; selected archive views retain a small physical sightline to the lower mechanism, rather than rendering another page.

Each show owns a compact stack of its real season/version plates behind its leading plate. A visible side lip and small page edges advertise that stack. Activating the lip fans that show's plates enough to select a version. This accommodates all 13 current archive previews without adding shelves or inventing content. Opened work approaches a planar screen and uses the real iframe. Closing restores the precise rig/camera pose and the initiating semantic link.

### Asset production and material

Author the folded arms, bent elbows, clevises, axle assemblies, counterweight and fractured stone as proper meshes. Static GLB assets are acceptable with a locally vendored loader; authoring assets offline does not add a site build step. Procedural construction is acceptable only if it produces these bevels, continuous sections and mechanically plausible joints. A group of generic boxes/cylinders is insufficient.

Brushed steel needs directional scratches in its roughness/normal maps, broad polished bevels and coherent reflected architecture. Surface scratches do not replace the broad reflected window. The clock face is pale satin steel, visibly softer than the counterweight face; its ink is near-black, tall and sharply legible. Cables are dark braided material. The stone has a fractured macro silhouette plus mineral chips, fine roughness and local seam dirt. The floor is pale polished stone with broad, subdued reflections and minute unevenness; it must reflect the weight's mass without turning into a mirror.

Construct an actual off-camera room and window reflection source: pale walls, dark window mullions, broad daylight openings and a darker lower band. Capture/prefilter this static environment once. Its orientation must agree with the visible window light on the floor. Moving the pointer must not rotate the entire environment map as a substitute for reflective geometry. Material roughness and bevels control where reflected bars appear.

### Lighting, shadows and effects

A broad high window at upper-left supplies warm-neutral daylight; cooler room bounce gives readable shaded sides. Use a single tightly bounded shadow light as a real-time approximation of the window, with restrained soft contact shading for the base, filings and weight. The source's diagonal window bands continue across wall and floor, not only a transparent bright floor decal. Reflection cards/room geometry supply chrome highlights separately from the diffuse light.

No bloom, general vignette, neon outlines or automatic chromatic effects. The camera sees the entire mechanism sharply. Gentle floor reflection and local ambient occlusion are the relevant effects. Dark cavities belong in axle sockets and folds; broad exterior surfaces must not be covered by dirty AO.

### Visible interaction and motion

Hover or keyboard focus on Again produces a small rotation/compression of its action face while the cylinder stays visibly heavy. Pending holds that pressure; errors release it with no transfer of energy. Confirmed success releases a small counterweight descent, turns the connected pulley, tensions the arms, and causes the four plates to lag and settle in a physically consistent order. Their numeral reel textures spin using the existing numerical model. The arm response is slow and weighty, not a shared bounce spring. Remote resets produce only a smaller cable/plate impulse.

Filings live on the actual floor, projected through the camera. Passing the cursor over the patch gathers/alines nearby filings toward a short-lived magnetic field; moving away leaves a narrow ridged trace that relaxes within a bounded interval. The patch is visible at rest so visitors can discover the interaction. A touch hold on the same patch and a native decorative-action equivalent produce the same response. This interaction never resets time. The sole crown is a small machined mark under a hinge flange, exposed by deliberately turning that flange; focus frames it and offers the same reveal.

### Phone composition

Keep the same mechanism and material, not a vertical rack of separate cards. Frame the lower arm and four clock plates broadly across the upper-middle; bring the weight closer in screen space through a genuine rig/camera pose. Show one upper archive plate with the next plate's side edge visible. A horizontal swipe moves that upper rail while the clock remains in the composition. A compact view of the base/floor makes the filings available; selecting it approaches the floor rather than replacing it with a new rectangular tray. Clock legibility and a 44 px Again target determine camera distance. At 320 px use two adjacent clock plate pairs on a folded lower arm only if a single row cannot stay readable; the rig remains connected.

### Sequencing and acceptance gate

First build the room, stone, one folded arm section, one full clock plate and the weight, with actual digits/actions on the meshes. Match the resting reference's metal, scale and depth before making filings or archive navigation. Gate this vertical slice with a reference/render comparison and a short moving-camera capture: broad reflection must change across rounded metal; plate ink must remain attached to its rotating face; the weight must cast and reflect a coherent contact. Reject frontal overlay numbers, painted-grey chrome, toy round pivots or a cylindrical body with a detached browser-facing button.

Only after that gate, complete both arms, four plates and three opening archive exhibits. The complete opening composition must resemble the source before extending the archive rail to all shows/versions. Then add the kinetic rig, filings and secret. Functional validation follows independently. Passing reset/API tests cannot approve this visual direction.

## Tomorrow's Roadworks

Reference: [original miniature](../references/tomorrows-roadworks.jpg). Rejected result: [material revision](../qa/tomorrows-roadworks-material-desktop.jpg).

### Reference commitments and current failure

The source is a dense, coherent miniature construction coast. A substantial cobalt road enters from the lower-left, makes a foreground bend, crosses behind the clock and rises in large loops to the upper-right. Its seven roadside exhibitions inhabit that same route. Large cast-concrete clock/action structures occupy upper-left, while a crane feeds an unfinished road from a suspended roll. Orange machinery, lane paint, rails, piers and local plant growth all establish one miniature scale.

The current rendered photographic coast supplies almost all richness, while the interactive foreground is a sparse toy blockout. A narrow strip crosses unrelated round rocks; generic large card rectangles sit in front of it. The clock is an oversized foreground UI panel, not an element in the same construction landscape. The current road's depth/width and lack of road-to-terrain relationships cannot be repaired by asphalt noise.

### Scene and composition

Replace the photographic foreground/coast with a complete 3D terrain and highway set. Distant sky can be a sky texture; the visible coast, quarry columns and ground that touches roads/piers must be geometry. Author fractured salmon limestone stacks with stratified ledges, broad irregular faces and localized rubble. Road supports emerge from or stand on actual ledges. Roads have continuous distance UVs, visible slab thickness, continuous guardrails, lane marks and joint seams; the lower edge must stay distinct from the blue driving surface.

Match the route's source silhouette before populating props: a wide entrance at lower-left, a sweeping foreground S-bend, a middle traverse under/behind the clock, and at least two increasing-height loops at the right. The clock rests upper-left on substantial blue pipe legs, not in the entire center of the view. Three curved work lamps visibly illuminate its black face. The separate action pillar connects mechanically/electrically to that structure. Its deep orange cap has a real rounded reflective surface and an inset printed/engraved Again; tally is on the column below. A crane and roll occupy the upper middle behind the action.

The opening composition shows seven show stations at unequal distances along that route. Foreground stations have larger supported screens; far ones are smaller but selectable. Physical number stones establish route order. Include only details that explain the miniature's construction: bolted piers, truss-supported screens, lane cones at merges, a parked truck beside a turn, hollow stacked pipes and localized scrub in cracks. Do not scatter random boulders to fill space.

### Camera, navigation and archive

Use a long-lens perspective miniature camera or a carefully tilted orthographic camera; choose by comparing the road's near/far scale against the original. The reference must determine the opening projection. A wide fixed overview is the home composition. Dragging empty ground or using wheel/trackpad follows a spline parallel to the blue route, with authored stops at each show. It does not scroll into a HTML continuation. Clicking a visible number stone or a native show link travels directly to that station; camera travel stops immediately for reduced motion.

Each station contains a leading real preview billboard. Shows with multiple versions have adjacent folding/hinged billboards arranged like construction sign stacks, with a visible second edge and a simple mechanical latch to fan them. Seven stations contain all 13 previews. Activating a version frames that real screen, then opens the existing preserved-page iframe. The billboard angle and screen origin come from the actual mesh, not an upright rectangular DOM card. Closing returns to the same road position. Walking/driving game controls are unnecessary; this concept is a miniature route examination, distinct from the Fair's third-person model.

### Asset production and material

Create an authored terrain asset with a low-frequency sculpted silhouette and UV-bearing foreground meshes. Build a reusable modular kit for road slab, pier, cast sign body, rails, truss, crane lattice, truck/roller/excavator and pipes. Model the recognisable machinery silhouettes; an eight-box orange vehicle is not an acceptable final truck. Bake localized AO into static construction assets where helpful, with original UVs and bounded texture atlases. Static asset authoring can use offline tools; the site remains served verbatim.

Terrain and precast concrete are related but visibly different materials: broken porous limestone versus fine sand-cast faces with seams and worn bevels. Road aggregate is cobalt with fine light flecks, rough/dusty edges and physical white paint. Orange enamel catches concentrated highlights, while tires remain matte with real tread. Steel rail/crane members need restrained directional reflections. Small vegetation is instanced but has authored species clusters and scale variation; it grows at limestone seams, not uniformly at random.

### Lighting, shadows and effects

One warm late-afternoon key from upper-left/front creates long consistent miniature shadows; pale blue sky fill leaves structure readable. Three work lamps add a localized warm clock-face glow, with actual shade silhouettes and bright interiors. Do not use full-scene bloom. A restrained emissive lamp highlight is sufficient.

Use tight shadow bounds for the actual terrain/road set, contact AO under supports/machinery and a subtle distance haze only on the far coast. The entire manipulable route remains sharp; optional depth-of-field may soften only distant noninteractive geometry and must be disabled around approached previews. Avoid imitating the source's miniature photography with a screen blur that also blurs useful archive content. Foreground and background must share exposure, light direction and texture scale.

### Visible interaction and motion

The road roll is a thick layered cylinder suspended through a real harness; its ribbon is one connected deformable slab. Pulling the roll or lifting its edge flexes that attached ribbon and rotates the harness; release settles with rubbery construction mass. This manipulation is decorative and never changes the deadline. The underside holds the sole crown, accessible through a real lift action and equivalent native focus/activation.

Again compresses only its orange cap during pending. After confirmed success, the roll turns, a finite length of new road feeds into the existing route, supports briefly telescope beneath it, and a single truck crosses the newly placed segment before parking behind structure. The old loop visibly becomes a slightly longer detour, making postponement legible. Use a bounded two-state route morph/hidden return segment rather than allocating a longer world after every reset. Errors retract the cap without starting the road event. Remote changes warm one work lamp and produce one short cable twitch.

### Phone composition

The opening phone camera includes the large clock/action, a foreground road entry and one clearly exposed station, with the continuing cobalt route receding upward. The terrain remains volumetric. Swipe/wheel follows the same route to each station, with direct physical number-stone selection available. Reposition the overview camera and a small number of authored structures rather than flattening all billboards into a vertical three-card list. The route can use a tighter phone curve; piers and station supports move with it. All 13 versions remain reachable, with legible real preview frames on approach.

### Sequencing and acceptance gate

First build one complete foreground bend, two supports, adjacent terrain, one station billboard and one modeled roller under the final lights. No photographic foreground is allowed in this gate. It must read as one detailed miniature, with terrain contact, continuous thick road/rails and faithful preview texture attached at the correct angle. Compare this crop with the corresponding original crop; reject toy smooth blocks, giant UI-card faces or a richly rendered distant image concealing a weak foreground.

Then author the full route silhouette and clock/action structures, and compare the entire opening composition before adding clutter. Integrate all stations/versions next, then the road-feed choreography and details. Roadworks has substantially more asset work than Metal; do not begin it by reusing the rejected generic three-mount scaffold.

## Still Drawing Tomorrow

Reference: [original animator's table](../references/still-drawing-tomorrow.jpg). Rejected result: [material revision](../qa/still-drawing-tomorrow-material-desktop.jpg).

### Reference commitments and current failure

The source is an overhead still life made of overlapping transparent acetate, graphite, warm worn paper, registration holes, paper tabs and a battered eraser. Its large clock is drawn on one broad cel; numerals contain actual graphite irregularity and red/blue construction drawings. The archive is a loosely overlapping folio, with a dramatically rolled-back corner and visible lower tabs. The runner is recognisable hand-drawn anatomy around an unfinished loop, not a generic stick figure. The eraser's material is rubbed, dusty and physically worn.

The current result is a scrapbook-like three-card row over a flat paper texture. The live screenshot rectangles ignore cel rotation; the cel curl is only a small triangulated white corner that does not refract/reflect its surroundings. The runner has elementary line limbs instead of the original animation drawing, numerals are uniform digital typography under a noise mask, and the eraser is a clean rounded web button. An overhead camera is faithful; the wrong surfaces, drawing assets and folio interaction are not.

### Scene and composition

Build one overhead desk, slightly skewed in perspective, with authored physical paper and acetate surfaces. A single large clock cel lies across the lower-middle. Three leading archive cels overlap across the upper half, with unequal widths/angles and visible underneath folios. Each leading cel belongs to a show folio; the complete seven-show archive lives as connected neighboring paper stacks around the table, not as HTML cards below it. The worn eraser sits lower-left; the running path curves beneath the clock toward the right; two pencils and a cropped mug can establish scale only after the sheets feel correct.

Separate ink, underlying paper and acetate. The real countdown is an ink texture on the cel surface, driven by the shared numerical model. Registration guides stay beneath it. The faithful archive thumbnail is similarly an ink/image layer on its cel, attached to the same UV surface and transform. A curled corner bends the sheet and its image together if it crosses that image. There cannot be an opaque DOM screenshot floating above a bending transparent sheet.

### Camera, navigation and archive

Use an orthographic or very long-lens near-overhead camera, a small fixed tilt sufficient to reveal sheet edges, and no orbit. The resting frame should preserve the original unequal overlapping composition, not regularize it into three columns. Dragging free desk space pans between authored folio positions; wheel/trackpad moves along a short table path. Only the selected group is approached for readability. An exposed show tab selects its folio and brings it physically onto the open desk region. Show names on tabs are sufficient; no navigation toolbar is required.

Each show folio contains its actual versions as layered cels. Protruding version tabs and paper edges indicate the number of sheets. A deliberate corner-lift action reveals/fans those tabs without opening a preview. Activating a cel's image opens its genuine archived-page iframe through the existing modal. This division keeps “lift to explore” separate from “open this work.” Native anchors/buttons offer the same operations and camera framing. Closing restores the folio layering and focused version exactly. All 13 current previews are available within the folios.

### Asset production and material

Author large curl silhouettes with a ruled/developable strip or a properly subdivided sheet mesh, including a moving boundary between flat and bent regions. A squared plane displaced only at its final corner is not enough. The rolled corner must show underside, edge thickness and a soft contact shadow beneath it. Separate the acetate layer from its paper backing by a physically small but visually useful gap; keep transparent overlaps bounded. Use a coherent bright window reflection on glossy acetate and fine scratches/scuffed edge roughness. Main sheets remain matte warm paper with fibers and worn stains at a plausible scale; the stock texture must not repeat visibly like wallpaper.

Commission/author the actual drawn assets before coding their animation: a twelve-pose runner with torso, bent limbs and consistent proportions, red/blue numeral construction outlines for the full digit set, irregular graphite fill and rough handwritten title/tally marks. SVG or raster atlases are appropriate because these are drawings, but they must be original finished drawings rather than elementary canvas line sketches. Number glyph atlases may be generated from a carefully selected tall condensed font and then authored with graphite pressure variations; the real reel selects/moves those glyphs. Do not bake any live value into art.

The eraser mesh has rounded worn corners, uneven rubber, a wrinkled paper sleeve and localized residue. Use a separate red-rubber surface, fibrous sleeve roughness, rubbed macro patches, fine nicks and bounded crumbs. Its printed Again belongs on the sleeve. The tally is real red ink beside it, with grouping strokes derived from the shared press count, bounded to a small selected display region instead of unlimited marks. Pencils have hexagonal painted wood shafts, cut wood cones and graphite tips, with actual curled shavings only near them.

### Lighting, shadows and effects

A broad warm window from upper-left creates soft daylight and a couple of broad diagonal mullion shadows across free desk space. White room bounce keeps the clock paper warm and legible. Use tight contact shadowing under sheets, eraser and curled corners; no grey dirt blanket. The glossy acetate reflects the same window layout. Transparent planes must not cast opaque rectangular shadows. Transmission/refraction is optional if it behaves reliably; a carefully authored reflection/roughness response and genuine curl is preferable to unstable expensive refraction.

No bloom, vignette, chromatic fringe or cursor particle fountain. Graphite can have a faint pressure trail only on free paper; it must be clipped away from artwork/action regions and fade quickly. The visible reference character comes from real drawing, light and sheet construction, not a postprocessing filter.

### Visible interaction and motion

Corner curling begins where a visible curled/lifted lip already exists. Hover raises it slightly; touch/native activation holds it open. Sheet image, holes, ink and specular reflection move together. Moving over a tab affects only that tab rather than moving its click target. Keyboard focus adds an authored pencil underline and frames the tab.

Again makes the eraser visibly press into the paper and hold during pending. Confirmed success slides it across the finish stripe, removes that stripe through an actual bounded ink mask, and leaves a rubbed patch/short dust trail. A pencil stroke redraws the finish farther along the same closed path. The runner advances with twelve discrete drawn poses, pauses before the new finish and holds. The connected eraser movement and ink removal must be visible in one composition; merely fading a stripe while a stationary button compresses is not the gag. Errors release pressure and leave the drawing intact. Remote updates alter one restrained runner pose.

The sole crown is a graphite drawing beneath a lifted cel, visible only when the corner is deliberately held open. Its native activation offers the same discovery and line. It is not permanently stuck above the sheet where the original lower layer should hide it.

### Phone composition

Frame the broad live clock cel first, with the worn eraser and visible runner finish below it. Keep one archive folio entering from the top edge and the next show's protruding tab visible. Selecting tabs pans to an archive reading composition; returning to the clock uses a physical registration tab. Do not turn the folio into a vertical feed of flat thumbnails. Each sheet remains recognisably material with true curl and overlap at 320/390 px. Simplify secondary props and onion-skin frames, not the graphite drawing or acetate construction. The key erase/redraw gesture remains visible; the previous implementation incorrectly removed the runner gag altogether on phones.

### Sequencing and acceptance gate

First produce one faithful archive cel with a broad curled corner, one large clock numeral region with real animated glyphs/registration ink, and the worn eraser under final daylight. Compare close crops with the original at rest, then curl the sheet: screenshot/ink must follow the same sheet transform, reflections must travel over the curl, and the underside/contact must establish acetate rather than a CSS card. Reject a tiny white corner triangle, uniform perfect numerals with random noise, stock-font title as the only handmade detail, and a clean red button masquerading as the source eraser.

Then finish the entire overhead opening composition and actual runner drawing. Only after this image matches the source's density/material/gesture, implement folio navigation and all real pages. Add erase/redraw motion, graphite trail and the secret last. A simple authored table can be less complex than a navigable world, but its shallow depth demands much stronger surface and drawing work.

## Architecture decision and order

Start with **Held in Suspense** as the new shared-adapter proof. Its rigid mesh surfaces and restrained camera make attachment, reflection, semantic focus and preview fitting measurable without hiding geometry behind generated background art. It tests the failed boundary directly. Do not use that rigid architecture to dictate the other concepts' navigation: Roadworks needs a spline miniature route; Drawing needs planar folios and deforming cels.

The first architecture milestone is a real scene containing one complete physical clock/action surface, one archive screen, correctly fitted true content, semantic focus, graphics-loss restoration and coherent final lighting. Its rendered original-reference comparison must pass before populating entire scenes. The next milestone is the faithful opening composition for the chosen concept, followed by its full archive and motion. This sequence is deliberately different from implementing seven generic layouts first and negotiating fidelity afterward.

## Shared rejection criteria

- A generated art layer supplies the detailed foreground while manipulable objects remain simplistic.
- Screen/digit text remains frontal while its material surface rotates or curls.
- Three decorative mounts above conventional themed shelves substitute for the reference's whole exhibition.
- Every concept inherits the same hero camera, click model or success bounce.
- Microtexture parameters are presented as evidence that a missing sculptural silhouette has been achieved.
- Phone layouts discard the concept's defining interaction in favor of a flat card feed.
- Functional tests or previous permissive “interpretation” reports are used to overrule the user's rejection.
