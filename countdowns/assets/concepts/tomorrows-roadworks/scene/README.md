# Tomorrow’s Roadworks

A full miniature coastal model with a thick continuous folded asphalt ribbon,
aged markings/guardrails, broken structural scenery, unequal planted outcrops,
clock worksite and a winding road roll. All thirteen preserved works occupy
physical surfaces; seven real archive/timeline/live destinations remain attached
to the same world. Clock and action ink are blank source faces filled by the
shared authoritative state, not printed into scenery.

The editable authoring source is authoring/scene.blend. Reproduction, coordinate
conversion, semantic node names and optimization details are in
[authoring/README.md](authoring/README.md). Blender 4.5.14 LTS is the selected
offline tool. No website build step or new runtime decoder is needed.

The final export pipeline preserves semantic faces and finite feed morphs.
KHR_mesh_quantization packs normal/tangent/UV attributes while positions and
morph displacements retain floats. Secondary relief is optimized after its
silhouette is preserved. An unoptimized fresh Blender export is not the final
runtime budget. The exact current file/hash/triangle/image measurements are in
the [shared asset report](../../../../../docs/countdown-concepts/architecture/asset-budgets.json).
Rerun its ordinary-Python measurement helper after any subsequent export.

Phone is independently staged by the runtime from this complete model. Coarse
aggregate, chipped castings, irregular stone and planted mass remain real shape;
microscopic maps do not replace them. Original mineral-coast.jpg is a retained
generated art reference from an earlier approach, not the rebuilt world’s photo
backdrop. Its recorded generation descriptor remains in ../README.md.

Geometry/procedural maps are project-authored without a new license declaration
here. The included Roboto Condensed font keeps typography/OFL-RobotoCondensed.txt
and its official Google Fonts source attribution. Shared Three.js retains its
MIT notice. Offline proofs and successful export counts remain separate from
actual desktop/390/320 light, usability, reduced-motion and preview-return gates.
